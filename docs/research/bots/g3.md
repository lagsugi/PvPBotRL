# G3 sword-duel experiment

G3 learns from random weights in the corrected 26.1.2 simulator. The bundled recurrent PPO, G2 and G2+ play as opponents and evaluation controls. The actor receives the local client's observation. The simulator supplies authoritative HP for reward and evaluation.

## Design

The previous three actors share four categorical action heads and a 64-unit GRU. G2 and G2+ continued an existing policy. Their terminal-corrected HP potential gives zero intermediate credit for equal simultaneous damage. The G2+ campaign won 98.98% of direct games against G2 but lost 0.0995 HP on average in common-opponent games both policies won. This motivates measuring damage exchanges and conditioning HP comparisons on the same matchup and seed. [G2+ experiment](recurrent-g2-plus-plan.md)

| Component | G3 |
| --- | --- |
| Actor | A 48-input encoder and recurrent state shared by the actor, critic and a training-only two-output forecast head. The first 44 values keep delivered HP, packet ages, kinematics, local action timing and previous submitted action. Inputs 44–47 are signed reach margin in blocks, radial closing speed, tangential relative speed and reach margin extrapolated three ticks at the current closing speed. |
| Forecast | Predict whether each player's delivered HP will drop over the next eight decisions. Mask windows crossing a match boundary or rollout cutoff. The auxiliary binary cross-entropy has coefficient 0.15. The head is omitted from deployed weights. |
| Objective | Win +1, loss or timeout −1, plus 0.65 times the surviving HP fraction on a win. Add terminal-corrected HP and approach potentials. Charge 0.45 times the overlapping exchanged damage divided by the actor's starting HP. The guided run also charges 0.00006 per decision and 0.65 extra for a timeout. A decisive trade can still earn a positive match return. |
| Exchange measurement | The first HP loss opens an eight-decision window. Flush at the window end or terminal decision. Overlap is the smaller of the damage dealt and taken. An even exchange has at least 75% as much damage on the smaller side as the larger side. A clean exchange deals positive damage and takes zero. |
| Cold-start exploration | Start with forward and swing logit biases. The guided run adds supervised targets for closing distance and making charged swings on the learner's own trajectory. Its coefficient falls linearly from 0.45 to zero in 400 updates. Lateral movement and jumping are learned throughout. |
| Opponent selection | After 20 curriculum updates, sample built-in opponents, the three recurrent controls and recent G3 snapshots. Within each pool, retain uniform probability mass and favor matchups with intermediate success. Three entire opponents are held out. Random spawns, seat assignment, frame phases and network variation use the existing scenario generator. |
| Optimization | Rust rollout, PyTorch recurrent PPO on the Radeon 780M, 384 lanes, rollout 128, sequences of 32 with up to eight burn-in decisions, 4,096-token minibatches and up to three passes per update. Reset both players' memory and previous action at match start. |

## Baseline measurements on November 24, 2026

Played 24 seat pairs per network condition for each ordered pair of the three bundled models (5,616 games). Seed family 628543, fixed equipment, 6,000-decision limit and deterministic actions. In mirror games each policy won half its matches. Fractions below are averaged per match. A repeated even window immediately follows another even window.

| Mirror opponent | Even windows | Repeated even windows | Mean longest even streak | Mean HP of winner |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| PPO | 27% | 15% | 4.08 | 0.95 |
| G2 | 39% | 19% | 4.12 | 1.70 |
| G2+ | 68% | 51% | 9.17 | 0.97 |

G2+ had the strongest repeated-trade pattern of the three in this sample. A mirror win rate of 50% alone cannot distinguish them. The full raw pairs and the nine matchup summaries from this run are under `build/g3/baseline-trades/`.

## Run and resume

Prepare the [780M Python environment](next-gen-gru-run.md#gpu-training-on-the-radeon-780m). Run from the repository root. The optional `-Python` parameter accepts an existing compatible GPU environment's Python executable. The launcher includes the release build in its active runtime budget.

```powershell
.\tools\research\recurrent-ppo\run-g3.bat -Quick -Output build/g3/smoke
.\tools\research\recurrent-ppo\run-g3.bat -Output build/g3/full
.\tools\research\recurrent-ppo\run-g3.bat -Output build/g3/full -Resume
```

The 24-hour launcher reserves the last hour for selection and evaluation. An active-time ledger is updated every five seconds and excludes time spent stopped or in Windows sleep. Evaluation may finish before the limit. Resume uses the remaining active budget, restores the optimizer, league, RNG and model, and starts new episodes. Training publishes an atomic checkpoint at least every five minutes while updates continue. Candidate weights and their matching checkpoints are saved every 30 minutes. The last weights match `latest.pt`.

## Evaluation

Development ranks up to eight evenly spaced snapshots and the last policy against all three recurrent controls and three training rivals on mixed, high-latency and asymmetric connections. The development score is win rate plus 0.35 times win-weighted HP fraction, minus 0.12 times even-exchange fraction, plus 0.05 times opening diversity, minus the timeout rate. Freeze the selected hash before the final test.

The full final design uses 128 paired seeds in each of 13 network conditions, keeping physical network assignments fixed during the seat swap. Each control plays direct games against G3. Each also plays the same seven opponents as G3, including the held-out G4 challenger, Reach and Orbit/Pressure. This totals 149,760 games. Opponents keep their normal deterministic actions. Each seed contributes both seats to the same bootstrap cluster. Intervals are marginal 95% intervals from 500 resamples stratified by condition and opponent.

| Outcome | Criterion against each of PPO, G2 and G2+ |
| --- | --- |
| Wins | Direct win-rate lower bound above 50% and a positive lower bound on the equal-cell common-opponent win gain. |
| Remaining HP | Positive lower bounds on both win-weighted HP gain (zero on losses and timeouts) and HP gain when both models win the same game. |
| Exchanges | Lower even-exchange and repeated-even fractions and higher clean-exchange fraction, with all three intervals excluding zero. Report fractions per match with zero for games without contact. |
| Tactical variety | Higher diversity of movement at the start of profitable exchanges within a match. Use six forward/neutral/back × lateral/straight categories. Estimate the probability that two distinct profitable openings differ, using zero when fewer than two exist. This measures a specific movement repertoire. |
| Response to an unfamiliar opponent | Against the held-out Orbit/Pressure bot, compare net damage dealt in the first 12 decisions after each actual style change, normalized by observed exposure. Require a positive lower bound on the gain and a switching-opponent win-rate lower bound of at least −1 percentage point relative to each control. This measures the behavioral response in the simulator. |
| Stability | Worst common-opponent network win-rate gain at least −2 percentage points and mean candidate timeout rate below 1%. |

The simulator does not supply human play evidence. The switching test can also reward a robust fixed tactic. The movement-category measure cannot establish that a player would perceive two actions as different strategies. Interpret both alongside direct wins, HP, clean exchanges and the per-cell results.

The evaluation directory contains frozen selection and model hashes, matching checkpoint provenance, deterministic Rust replays, raw seat pairs and the final summary with every decision criterion. The short workflow always sets `accepted=false`. Preserve candidates under `build/`; an experimental result and a release are separate decisions.

## G3 Prototype on Paper

Paper exposes the selected update 18,983 as `g3-prototype`. It uses the evaluated weights and the `g3` feature schema. [Weight provenance](../../../models/bundled/g3_prototype.source.json)

In initial player testing, the player reported that the prototype opened and extended combos, with a narrower set of effective responses than G2+. The player found ways to beat it within the initial testing session. Those observations came from one player and motivate testing future candidates against human opponents who change spacing and timing in response to the bot.

In the simulator's fixed-equipment, seat-swapped direct evaluation against G2+, the prototype won 250/256 at 0 ms, 245/256 at 2 ms, and 230/256 at 20 ms symmetric RTT. The three existing local Paper match records for the same matchup all placed the prototype in seat A, recorded 20 ms RTT for each bot, and gave G2+ the win after 553 active ticks with 2.46 HP remaining. The Paper runtime config controls NPC latency independently of the spectator's connection. In the normal Paper input path, attack and movement actions are queued against a 50 ms tick clock, so a positive uplink delay below 50 ms can move application to the following tick.

The November 25, 2026 reproduction used isolated Paper 26.1.2 build 74 with Citizens build 4270, the Example practice arena, a 20-block starting gap, and the normal Bot vs Bot input path. The match records confirmed the configured RTT and weights hash for each bot. Both physical seats were tested equally.

| Symmetric RTT | Paper equipment | Prototype wins / matches | G2+ HP by prototype seat A / B |
| ---: | --- | ---: | --- |
| 0 ms | Normal durability | 0/6 | 3.84 / 2.19 |
| 0 ms | Fixed equipment | 0/6 | 3.84 / 2.19 |
| 2 ms | Fixed equipment | 0/4 | 2.46 / 1.16 |
| 20 ms | Fixed equipment | 0/4 | 2.46 / 1.16 |

All Paper matches ended by death. Repetitions within each seat and condition produced identical outcomes, durations, and final HP. These results cover one opening and arena. In a Rust follow-up using a fixed 20-block opening, PaperFlatV2 rules, fixed equipment, and base seed 938765123, the prototype won 64/64 at each of 0, 2, and 20 ms. Matching the starting gap and equipment therefore preserved the outcome reversal. The exact divergence in live observation or action processing remains unlocalized. The shared-observation test covered 800 episodes across the four bundled models and matched features, logits, recurrent state, and selected actions.

Raw Paper records are in `build/g3/paper-smoke/latency-results.jsonl`. The fixed-opening Rust configuration and summaries are in `build/g3/paper-smoke/rust-fixed-opening/`.

### First divergence and critical-hit audit

Audited the same 20-block opening and fixed equipment on November 25, 2026. The two 0 ms and two 2 ms Paper matches used the normal Bot vs Bot path and swapped seats. Rust used PaperFlatV2, base seed 938765123, first client seat A, and the arena's initial coordinates and orientations with the floor at Y=0. Compared policy inputs at the decision boundary and attack conditions immediately before the server handles a selected target.

| Symmetric RTT | Prototype seat | Prototype accepted crits, Rust / Paper | G2+ accepted crits, Rust / Paper | Winner HP, Rust prototype / Paper G2+ |
| ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 ms | A | 3 / 5 | 2 / 16 | 15.65 / 3.84 |
| 0 ms | B | 2 / 4 | 4 / 16 | 13.53 / 2.19 |
| 2 ms | A | 9 / 4 | 11 / 12 | 0.58 / 2.46 |
| 2 ms | B | 9 / 1 | 13 / 11 | 0.72 / 1.16 |

The prototype's first damaging hit was critical in every audited match in both engines. In the 0 ms seat A match, Paper reported 10.5 raw damage and 1.1529 final damage. All 180 Paper damage events agreed with the flat-arena critical condition evaluated from the attacker immediately before packet handling, using charge above 0.9, ground and sprint flags, and positive fall distance. Attempts with no damage event are excluded. In the 2 ms Paper runs, the prototype had 18 selected attacks that were charged, airborne, descending with positive fall distance, and still sprinting. These reached the handler in a state that excludes critical hits.

At 0 ms, the first observed difference was the remote grounded flag at round decision 0. The Paper pairing packet omits it and the replica holds its default false value until a movement update. Rust initializes the remote as grounded at a floor spawn. By decision 1, remote position and packet age differed while the bot's own motion still agreed. For example, G2+ in seat B saw 0.03263 blocks of the prototype's lateral movement in Paper and zero in Rust. This precedes the first attack. G2+ kept strafing left at decision 4 in Rust and moved straight forward in Paper. The prototype added forward movement at decision 19 in Rust and continued strafing without forward input in Paper.

In a recurrent replay of the captured Paper inputs, substituting only the Rust value of the initial remote grounded flag reproduced the G2+ action at decision 4 in both seats, leaving prior chosen actions the same. Substituting the Rust remote position, displacement and velocity history reproduced the prototype's action at decision 19 in both seats with prior actions intact. These interventions identify sufficient input changes for those individual decisions. They use recorded trajectories. A separate live intervention seeded the initial Paper remote grounded flag from the target and kept the normal runtime after pairing. G2+ still won both 0 ms seats, after 404 and 416 active ticks with 4.32 and 2.97 HP, respectively. The initial flag explains the first G2+ action divergence and does not recover the simulator result by itself.

The audit localizes an opening observation divergence and a later change in the kinds of attacks each bot gets to perform. It has not isolated the contributions of continued tracker timing, aim and target selection, sprint transitions, knockback, or damage immunity to the final reversal. The fixed opening and the two RTT settings are the scope of this audit.

The diagnostic scripts, temporary instrumentation patch, full Rust decision traces, Paper TSVs, outcome records, and recurrent input substitution results are under `build/g3/paper-smoke/`. The standard Paper and Example builds passed after removing the instrumentation.

### First exchange and recorded-action replay

At 0 ms, the prototype landed the first hit in both engines. In Paper, G2+ answered two ticks later with another fully charged critical in both seats. In the original Rust runs, its reply two ticks after first contact was a normal hit. Full-match counts showed no target-selection misses in the Paper 0 ms runs. G2+ dealt damage on 41 of 87 selected attacks in Paper and 12 of 13 server-handled attacks in Rust. The prototype's accepted sprint hits went from 50 in Rust to 32 in Paper. These counts distinguish the kinds of exchanges and the opportunities each model found. They come from separate closed-loop trajectories with different match lengths.

A second diagnostic replayed both Paper bots' recorded action sequences through Rust with the original 0 ms simulator setup, the same seats, and normal Rust target tracking. At decision 80 the same prototype click was within reach in Paper and outside reach in the Rust replay in both seats. The prototype's eye-to-replica-box distance was 2.633 blocks in Paper and 3.057 blocks in Rust. Paper produced a critical hit. Rust produced no attack for that click and G2+ landed the first accepted attack at server tick 83. Paper's opponent position was 50 ms old at this decision and Rust's was 100 ms old. The physical gap also differed. In seat A it was 2.380 blocks between the Paper bots and 2.525 blocks between the Rust local bodies. The replay exhausted the recorded inputs with both Rust bots still alive in both seats. This is an open-loop diagnostic, since Rust cannot choose new actions based on its resulting observations.

The replay demonstrates that identical submitted inputs can straddle the reach threshold across the runtimes. It does not reproduce the outcome of either free-running match. For reproduction, see `exchange-audit.py`, `exchange-audit.txt`, `paper-actions-*.json`, `duel-action-replay.rs`, `rust-replayed-paper-*.jsonl`, and `replay-opening.txt` under `build/g3/paper-smoke/`.

### Controlled outcome reversals on Paper

On November 27, 2026, the 0 ms reversal was reproduced and changed by single interventions in isolated Paper 26.1.2 build 74 with Citizens build 4270. The tests used the 20-block Practice opening, fixed equipment, the same model hashes and both seat assignments. Paper outcomes are from completed live duels with each bot continuing to choose its actions. The diagnostic build was based on commit e34d788.

| 0 ms Paper change | Prototype wins / matches | Winner HP with prototype in seat A / B |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Normal Citizens path | 0/2 | G2+ 3.84 / 2.19 |
| Apply the same raw victim impulse immediately before movement | 2/2 | Prototype 14.24 / 7.30 |
| Apply the packet-rounded impulse immediately in the attack handler | 0/2 | G2+ 4.31 / 3.40 |
| Apply the packet-rounded impulse immediately before movement | 2/2 | Prototype 14.24 / 10.57 |
| Use native self-motion packet delivery before NPC movement | 2/2 | Prototype 14.24 / 10.57 |
| Deliver live opponent tracker updates one extra decision later | 2/2 | Prototype 3.16 / 13.74 |
| Disable the critical bonus by clearing only G2+'s fall distance during its attack handler | 2/2 | Prototype 2.41 / 1.82 |
| Disable only the prototype's criticals in the same way | 0/2 | G2+ 2.35 / 2.81 |

**Knockback order.** In the normal Paper path, the paired bots apply actions in alternating order at the start of the server tick. The pinned Citizens `EntityHumanNPC.hurtServer` clears `hurtMarked` after accepted damage and schedules restoration of that flag. The normal NPC therefore retains the changed velocity immediately in its server body, and the second bot can read it in its policy decision in the same tick. The packet path restores the pre-hit body velocity and applies the decoded self-motion update before local movement. Rust commits both decisions before advancing the simulator. In the 0 ms seat-A opening, G3 hit at decision 80. G2+ read X velocity +0.27595 and pressed forward in normal Paper. Holding back the identical impulse until movement left G2+ reading its pre-hit X velocity −0.24796, and it pressed backward. The prototype's pre-hit inputs and the first damaging hit were unchanged. With raw deferral, G2+'s accepted hits dropped from 20/21 to 5/12 in the two seat assignments. The packet-rounded value alone did not change either winner.

**Tracker order.** Paper's tracker sent updates by tick end, and its NPC consumed them at the next decision. The first position update appeared at decision 1 on Paper and decision 2 in the baseline Rust trace. A 50 ms deadline on the live tracker feed still delivered on the normal Paper decision. A 100 ms deadline moved consumption one decision later while leaving the initial pairing and attack uplink alone. At decision 1 the prototype's observed relative Z changed from 19.95752 to 20.00000 blocks in seat A, matching the baseline Rust value at that decision. Before the first attack, G2+'s movement changed at decision 1 and the prototype's at decision 28. G2+'s accepted hits dropped to 16/6. This intervention shifts all live tracker updates; the individual position, velocity, metadata and health effects after the opening are not separated.

The extra G2+ critical damage was a causal contributor to its 0 ms wins. The first G2+ reply remained at decision 82 and its damage dropped from 1.1529 HP to 0.6804 HP when only that bot's criticals were disabled. In the isolated Rust build, suppressing only the prototype's criticals left it winning all four 0 ms runs spanning both seats and both same-time client orders. At 2 ms, the same Rust intervention moved it from four wins to zero. The contribution of criticals therefore depends on the runtime and timing.

The controlled reversals establish two sufficient changes for this opening. The first concerns the victim's own velocity at decision time. The second concerns the phase at which the opponent's tracker data is consumed. They can each change the winner while the other path keeps its normal behavior. The interventions are diagnostics for this matchup and opening, and do not establish whole-duel parity.

At 2 ms, the one-extra-decision tracker intervention also gave G3 Prototype both wins, with 15.18 HP in seat A and 4.11 HP in seat B. Raw knockback deferral gave one win and one loss, with G3 Prototype winning seat B with 3.50 HP and G2+ winning seat B with 2.73 HP. The unmodified 2 ms control gave G2+ both wins. The 20 ms profile was checked in the earlier baseline and was outside this intervention sweep.

The temporary Paper instrumentation and replay scripts are stored under `build/g3/paper-smoke/`. The files `cause-sweep-results.jsonl` and `two-ms-results.jsonl` contain the paired tracker and critical trials. `build/g3/cause-motion/` contains the self-motion and impulse-only results. The Rust phase and critical sweeps are in `build/g3/cause-rust/results.jsonl`. The standard Paper and Example builds passed after restoring the production sources.

### Closed-loop Rust reproduction

The `paper_citizens` driver now reproduces the Practice matchup in Rust. The tracker audit found two further differences after first contact. Paper sent both copies of the hit motion in the same tracker tick. Rust must also publish sprint metadata only when the server flag changes, including when the input sprint latch changes after a sprint hit has already cleared the server flag. Without that condition, a duplicate update changed the observed metadata age and the phase of position updates.
